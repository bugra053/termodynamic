PHYSICS CHECKS
- Establish the system/control volume and governing balance before substituting numbers. For a stationary closed system: Q_in-Q_out+W_in-W_out=m(u_2-u_1), if KE/PE changes are negligible.
- W_b,out=integral(P_boundary dV), positive in expansion. Compression input magnitude is W_b,in=-W_b,out; label 'in' or 'out'. Keep electrical, shaft and paddle-wheel work distinct from boundary work.
- Rigid means W_b=0, not necessarily total W=0. Insulated means Q=0, not zero work.
- A frictionless loaded piston requires a force balance, such as P=P_atm+(m_p g+F_spring)/A for opposing loads. Distinguish absolute and gage pressure.
- Constant-pressure boundary work is P(V_2-V_1). Stops and spring contact create separate stages. With constant loading, motion to stops is isobaric; after stops, dV=0 and boundary work is zero. Use average pressure only for an actually linear P-V segment.
- For a constant-pressure stationary closed system with the usual boundary work separated, Delta U+W_b,out=Delta H. Include electrical/shaft input and heat loss correctly without counting boundary work twice.
- Identify phase before selecting data. Quality is vapor mass fraction: x=(v-v_f)/(v_g-v_f); u=u_f+x*u_fg, h=h_f+x*h_fg. Quality applies only within saturation limits. Phase volume fraction is not quality.
- Compressed-liquid approximations require justification and a brief approximation label. Water and R-134a are different substances.
- Use absolute pressure and kelvin in PV=mRT. Ideal-gas Delta u and Delta h depend on temperature; choose Cengel specific heats or property data appropriate to the problem.
- For filling/draining or mixing, use the correct transient mass and energy balances with stream enthalpy. Do not assume steady state merely because flow rates are constant.
- m_dot=rho*A*c=rho*V_dot. Distinguish speed c, specific volume v, total volume V and volume flow V_dot.
- Electrical input U_e*I*Delta t with volts, amps and seconds is joules. Convert to kJ as needed; voltage U_e is not volume V.
- Conversions: 1 MPa=1000 kPa; 1 bar=100 kPa; 1 cm^2=10^-4 m^2; 1 L=10^-3 m^3; 1 min=60 s; 1 kPa*m^3=1 kJ; 1 kWh=3600 kJ. Distinguish kJ, kJ/kg and kW.
- For required diagrams show axes, states, direction and saturation region as applicable. On a text-only calculator, use a short path description. Never emit TikZ, PGF, picture environments, drawing code or code fences unless explicitly requested; inline mathematical LaTeX is sufficient.
